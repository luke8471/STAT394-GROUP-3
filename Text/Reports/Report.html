2. Introduction

2.1 Significance of this archaeological find and Gela

The historic Gela, Italy has been a treasure trove of archaeological finds for generations. This ancient city was strategically positioned at the crux of major maritime trade routes, which made it a populous and successful trading port (Angelucci, 2026). Consequently, ships looking to trade goods like pottery, metals, and grain have traveled to and from Gela for centuries.

A shipwreck off the coast continues to provide significant archaeological finds to this day. This discovery dates back to 1988 (Nutter, 2025), where it was first stumbled upon by divers near Bulala beach. We are exploring one of the most significant finds from this wreck; A veritable hoard of the fabled metal, Orichalcum.

The discovery of 40 orichalcum ingots in 2017 was a monumental one. There had never been a discovery of this quantity in one place anywhere in the world. There was also evidence of consistent casting processes, with overall similarities suggesting a rudimentary mold production. This was likely a hole dug in the ground and molten alloy was poured in the indentation to solidify. The morphology of the found ingots suggested cast-diverse technologies.

2.2 What is orichalcum?

Orichalcum is defined as a copper alloy with zinc content as the principal components. A widely varying quantity of lead and tin among other trace elements are expected. There is no consistent proportion to the amount of copper and zinc found, but the overall composition and relative amounts of principal elemental components can be used to deduce the rough date and origin of a sample. The modern day equivalent is brass.

2.3 Summary of Collection Techniques

In the initial analysis of the orichalcum ingots, X-Ray Fluorescence (XRF) spectroscopy via a portable spectrometer (Caponetti, Armetta, Martino, et al., 2017) was used. The subject paper includes that data and further investigates the composition of each ingot using more invasive and refined elemental analysis.

To obtain material for further testing, a micro-well was drilled into each ingot and the extracted alloy was taken to determine trace elements using Inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES) and Inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-MS) of each of the 38 orichalcum ingots. As you can see in table 2, our values are now all non-zero.

The normal QQ-plot looks less normal after standardisation, with the length of the tails being accentuated and continuing presence of stepped data.

The Henze-Zirkler test stipulates

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathcal{H}_0 :& \text{ The data follow a multivariate normal distribution}, \\ \mathcal{H}_1 :& \text{ The data do not follow a multivariate normal distribution}. \end{aligned} \]

This generated a p-value=0.069, suggesting that there is insufficient evidence to reject that the data follow a multivariate normal distribution. However, the QQ-plot shows enough <flat tails, step, steep, etc> that further testing is warranted before determining if multivariate normality is likely.

Given the uncertainty of multivariate normality and the presence of outliers, further data manipulation by Medioid Absolute Deviation was determined to be the next best step towards multivariate analysis.
